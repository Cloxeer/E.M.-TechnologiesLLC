07 — VOICE & TONE
Brand Voice
WHAT WE SOUND LIKE
• Direct: We say what we mean. Short sentences. Active
voice.
• Earned confidence: We let the work speak and stand
behind it firmly.
• Community-aware: Tech serves people, not the reverse.
• No jargon: Tech terms only when necessary, always
explained.
• Warm but firm: Approachable, not casual. Professional,
not cold.
WHAT WE DON'T SOUND LIKE
 Corporate filler: "leveraging synergies," "best-in-class
solutions"
 Overpromising: "We'll transform your business
overnight"
 Hollow tech buzzwords without substance
 Apologetic language — never "we try to" or "we hope
to"
 Dismissive of non-technical clients or small
communities
COPY EXAMPLES — BEFORE & AFTER
 BEFORE
 AFTER
"We leverage cutting-edge technologies to deliver
innovative digital transformation solutions."
"We build websites, apps, and digital systems that work —
designed for your community, maintained for the long haul."
 BEFORE
 AFTER
"Our team hopes to provide value-added services that may
potentially improve your online presence."
"We build your digital presence from the ground up — and we
stand behind every line of code."

09 — DEVIL'S ADVOCATE
Blind Spots to Watch
I Brand Without Proof
A great brand guide means nothing if your portfolio is thin. Fill that Work
section with real, documented results first. The brand amplifies the work — it
cannot replace it.
I Consistency Drift
The #1 brand killer. One off-brand social post, one wrong font, one logo on a
busy background — it chips away at trust. Treat this guide as law, not a
suggestion.
I Playing It Too Safe
This brand has real visual identity and cultural depth. Don't dilute it trying to
look like every other tech shop. Lean into what makes you distinctly E.M.
Technologies. Sameness is invisible.
I Neglecting Mobile
General tech clients often evaluate vendors on their phone first. If your site isn't
tight on mobile, you lose the contract before you ever send a proposal. Design
mobile first. Always.
I No Portfolio = No
Credibility
Get 3–5 strong documented case studies live before driving traffic. Each one
should show: the problem, your approach, the result with numbers. Most
important marketing asset you have.
I Forgetting the Long
Game
Brands compound over time like good soil. The discipline you show in years
1–2 determines what you're worth in years 5–10. Stay the course even when
it's tempting to cut corners.
Forward Perspective
The technology landscape shifts fast. This guide is a living document — revisit it every 12–18 months. But
your core identity? The roots, the precision, the community-first values — those don't change. Let those be
your anchor while everything else evolves around them.
